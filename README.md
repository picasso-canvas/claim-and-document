# Channel Constitution

Version 1.0 draft · As of 2026-11-12 · Not yet adopted

This document binds every person and every automated tool that produces content for the channel. A companion doc with the same text lives at https://claude.ai/artifact/Lkj6vdjWveH6Cpam8nLntY. If the two differ, this README is authoritative once adopted.

**Caveat.** No charter can make a channel immune under a law that has an "unverified information" offence with no public-interest defence. This constitution removes every avoidable trigger and documents a process that can be shown in defence. It is not legal advice and must be reviewed by a Bangladeshi advocate before adoption.

---

## 1. Purpose

This channel examines public statements by public office-holders and candidates in Bangladesh, and tests each statement against the public record and the rules of valid reasoning. It publishes on Facebook, YouTube and TikTok.

The channel does three things and only three things:

1. Quotes a specific public statement, exactly as spoken, with its date and source.
2. Compares the statement with primary documents and states where they differ.
3. Names the logical structure of an argument where the structure itself is invalid.

The channel does not judge character, motive, honesty, religion, family, or private life. It does not campaign for or against any party or candidate. It is not a news outlet and does not break stories; it analyses statements already on the public record.

Every person who writes, edits, presents or publishes for the channel agrees to this constitution before their first piece. A piece that breaks any article of it is not published, whoever made it and however strong the point.

## 2. Legal foundation

The channel operates under Article 39 of the Constitution of Bangladesh, which guarantees freedom of thought, speech and the press subject to restrictions imposed by law. Every rule below exists because one of the following laws can be used against a channel like this one.

| Law | What it reaches | Rule in this constitution that answers it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cyber Security Act (Ordinance 2025, enacted and amended 2026) | Online defamation, humiliation, disparagement, bullying; spreading unverified information; content the authority orders removed | Articles 3, 4, 5, 8, 12 |
| Penal Code sections 499 and 500 | Criminal defamation of any person, including public figures | Articles 3, 4, 11 |
| Penal Code sections 295A and 298, and the religious-sentiment provisions of the cyber law | Statements that hurt religious feeling | Article 8 |
| Contempt of Courts Act 2013 | Comment on pending cases, judges and court orders | Article 8 |
| Copyright Act 2023 | Use of broadcast and online footage | Article 9 |
| Representation of the People Order 1972 and Election Commission codes of conduct | Campaign-period rules on broadcast content, polling-day silence and false statements about candidates | Articles 7, 10 |
| Official Secrets Act 1923 and national-security provisions | Leaked documents, defence and intelligence matters | Articles 5, 8 |
| Draft National Media Commission ordinance | Possible registration or licensing of online media | Article 14 (review trigger) |

The cyber law as amended has no explicit public-interest or fair-comment defence. The channel therefore does not rely on any defence at trial. It relies on never producing the kind of content that gets charged: no claim without a documented source, and no statement about a person that is not a statement about a quoted public utterance.

This constitution is reviewed by a Bangladeshi advocate with cyber-law experience before launch and after every amendment to the cyber law. The advocate's name and the date of the last review are recorded in Article 14.

## 3. Editorial principles

Every piece follows eight rules. A piece that cannot meet one of them is not made.

1. **The claim, not the person.** The subject of every piece is a quoted statement. The channel never makes a statement about what a politician is, only about what a politician said and what the record shows.
2. **Quote exactly.** The statement is shown in the speaker's own words, with the original audio, in full sentence context. No paraphrase stands in for the quote. No cut that changes the meaning.
3. **Primary sources only.** A statement is called inaccurate only when a primary document contradicts it: a gazette, a budget, a court order, Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics data, Bangladesh Bank data, Election Commission results, parliamentary records, or the speaker's own earlier recorded words. A news report is a lead, not a source.
4. **Show the source on screen.** The contradicting document appears on screen with its title, issuing body, date and page, long enough to be paused and read.
5. **No motive, no intent.** The channel never says why a speaker said something. "Lied", "deceived", "misled" and their Bangla equivalents are prohibited because each asserts intent, which no document can prove.
6. **Say what is unknown.** Where the record is incomplete or disputed, the piece says so and stops. "Could not be verified" is a finding, not a failure.
7. **Same test for everyone.** The verification standard, the language rules and the fallacy standard apply identically to every party, office and speaker.
8. **If in doubt, cut.** A sentence that any reviewer flags as uncertain, unsourced or characterising is removed. There is no appeal from a cut; a point lost is cheaper than a case.

## 4. Language rules

The cyber law's defamation, humiliation and disparagement offences turn on wording, so wording is controlled. The table applies to scripts, captions, titles, thumbnails, on-screen text, pinned comments and replies to comments, in Bangla and English alike.

| Do not say | Say instead | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lied, liar, মিথ্যাবাদী, মিথ্যা বলেছেন | the statement is inaccurate; the record shows otherwise; তথ্যটি সঠিক নয় | asserts intent |
| corrupt, thief, looter, চোর, দুর্নীতিবাজ | the audit report (page N) records…; the court found… | characterises the person |
| fooling the public, deceiving voters, জনগণকে বোকা বানানো | the claim does not match the published figure | asserts motive |
| shameless, idiot, clown, incompetent, any insult or nickname | (no replacement; delete) | humiliation offence |
| propaganda, fake, fraud, scam | unsupported by the sources checked | loaded and unprovable |
| everyone knows, it is obvious, clearly | the following three documents show | invites an unverified-information charge |
| he always, she never, this party only ever | on [date], [date] and [date] the speaker said | generalisation without a source |
| rumour-style phrasing: reportedly, sources say, it is said that | (no replacement; delete the sentence) | rumour provision of the cyber law |
| any statement about health, family, marriage, religion, caste, ethnicity, sexuality or appearance | (never) | Article 8 |
| Hitler, fascist, traitor, রাজাকার, Indian agent, foreign agent | (never) | characterisation and communal risk |

Three further rules:

- **Verbs of finding, not verbs of accusation.** "The figure in the budget is X; the speaker said Y." Not "the speaker got it wrong".
- **Titles and thumbnails obey the same table.** A thumbnail that says "CAUGHT" or "EXPOSED" fails. A title states the topic: "Garment export figure, 12 Nov 2026 speech, checked against EPB data".
- **Mockery is not analysis.** No laughing, no sarcastic tone, no memes, no caricature, no altered image or voice of any person. Delivery is flat and even throughout.

## 5. Verification standard

A statement may be called inaccurate only when the verification file for the piece is complete. The file is the channel's defence against the unverified-information offence and is kept for every piece, published or not.

**Source hierarchy.** Sources are ranked. A finding rests on the highest available tier, and a tier-1 or tier-2 source is required before the word "inaccurate" is used.

| Tier | Source type | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Official document of the issuing authority | Gazette, Finance Division budget documents, Bangladesh Bank bulletins, BBS releases, Election Commission gazetted results, court judgments, parliamentary proceedings |
| 2 | The speaker's own recorded words | Earlier speech, interview, press conference, official social-media post, with full recording |
| 3 | Reports by bodies with a legal mandate | Comptroller and Auditor General, ACC published findings, Bangladesh Bank inspection reports, World Bank or IMF country documents |
| 4 | Established news reporting | Two independent outlets reporting the same fact from named sources; used only to locate a tier-1 to 3 source, never as the sole basis |

**Two-source rule.** Every factual finding is supported by at least two independent sources, at least one of them tier 1 or 2. A finding supported by one source is published as "the only available record shows", or not at all.

**No leaked or classified material.** The channel uses only documents already lawfully public. A document that reaches the channel privately is not used, whatever it shows, until it is published by its issuing authority or a mainstream outlet.

**Verification file.** For every piece the file contains:

- [ ] Original footage with URL, platform, upload date and the speaker's exact words transcribed in Bangla
- [ ] The specific claim being tested, written as one sentence
- [ ] Each source: title, issuing body, date, page or section, URL, and a saved PDF or screenshot with capture date
- [ ] The finding in one sentence, using Article 4 language
- [ ] Name and date of the researcher, the second checker and the legal reviewer
- [ ] Any point of doubt and how it was resolved or cut

**Numbers.** A figure is quoted with its unit, period and source definition. Where the speaker's figure and the document's figure measure different things (fiscal year versus calendar year, taka versus dollars, gross versus net), the piece says so and does not call the statement inaccurate on that difference alone.

## 6. Fallacy labelling standard

A fallacy label describes the structure of an argument, never the speaker. It is applied only when all four conditions hold:

1. The speaker made an argument, with a stated premise and a stated conclusion, not merely an assertion or a slogan.
2. The argument is quoted in full, so viewers can see the premise and conclusion themselves.
3. The invalid step can be shown in one sentence of the form "the conclusion does not follow because…".
4. A reasonable listener could not read the argument as rhetoric, emphasis or shorthand instead.

**Labels the channel uses.** The channel uses a fixed list of twelve, each with a Bangla name and a one-line definition shown on screen every time it is used: ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, hasty generalisation, post hoc, appeal to fear, appeal to popularity, slippery slope, whataboutism (tu quoque), false equivalence, moving the goalposts, and cherry-picking. A fallacy outside the list is described in plain words without a label.

**Labels the channel does not use.** "Gaslighting", "manipulation", "brainwashing" and similar terms assert intent and are prohibited. "Ad hominem" is used only for the structure of the speaker's argument; the channel itself never answers an argument by describing the person who made it.

**Rhetoric is not fallacy.** Exaggeration, repetition, emotional appeal, and attacks on a rival party's record are ordinary political speech. The channel labels them only where they stand in for a missing argument. The standard is: would a logic teacher mark this as invalid reasoning, or merely as persuasive?

**One label per argument.** Stacking several labels on one passage is prohibited. Choose the one that fits best, or none.

## 7. Balance and coverage

The channel covers statements from every party and office with the same test. Balance is measured, not assumed.

- **Selection by claim, not by speaker.** A statement is chosen because it is specific, checkable and widely circulated. It is never chosen because of who said it.
- **Quarterly audit.** Every quarter the channel publishes a count of pieces by party and office of the speaker, and the share found accurate, inaccurate and unverifiable. If one party exceeds half the pieces in a quarter, the next quarter's selection corrects it.
- **Accuracy gets covered too.** When a checked statement proves accurate, that piece is published with equal prominence. A channel that only publishes failures is a campaign, not a check.
- **Government and opposition alike.** The ruling party, the opposition, parties outside parliament, and figures in exile are all within scope. Office-holders receive more scrutiny than others only because they make more checkable claims about public money and policy.
- **Election periods.** From the announcement of an election schedule to the declaration of results, the channel follows the Election Commission's code of conduct for media, publishes nothing new in the 48 hours before polling, and applies a second legal review to every piece about a candidate.
- **No endorsements.** The channel, its presenters and its accounts never state a voting preference, share campaign material, accept party funding or advertising, or appear at party events.
- **Declared interests.** Every team member declares party membership, past employment with a party or government, and family ties to candidates. A member with a declared interest does not work on pieces about that party.

## 8. Prohibited subjects

The following are outside the channel's scope in every piece, caption, comment and reply. A statement by a politician on one of these subjects may be quoted only to check a separate factual claim inside it, and the prohibited element is not discussed.

| Subject | Why it is excluded |
| --- | --- |
| Religion, religious figures, scripture, and any speaker's religious practice | Religious-sentiment offences under the Penal Code and the cyber law carry the heaviest penalties and the lowest bar for complaint |
| Pending court cases, judges, and the merits of any court order | Contempt of Courts Act 2013 |
| The armed forces, intelligence agencies, and defence procurement | National-security provisions; Official Secrets Act |
| Private life: family, marriage, children, health, wealth not on the public record, appearance | Humiliation and disparagement offences; no public-interest defence |
| Ethnicity, caste, indigenous identity, migration status, sexuality, gender identity | Communal-harmony provisions; no analytical value to the channel's purpose |
| Anyone under 18, including politicians' children | Children's protection law and ordinary decency |
| The 1971 Liberation War and its historical disputes, beyond quoting a specific checkable figure | Contested history; a frequent complaint trigger even after the 2025 repeal of the specific sections |
| Leaked, hacked or privately supplied documents | Article 5; possible Official Secrets Act exposure |
| Foreign governments' intentions or alleged links between a speaker and a foreign state | Unprovable; "foreign agent" claims are characterisation |
| Calls to action of any kind: protest, boycott, vote, share, report | Turns analysis into campaigning |

Two further prohibitions:

- **No synthetic media.** The channel never alters a person's face, voice or words, including for satire, and never uses AI-generated images or audio of any real person. AI tools may be used for transcription and research only, and every AI output is checked against the source by a person before use.
- **No comments section as a loophole.** A presenter's reply to a viewer comment is channel content. Everything in Articles 3, 4 and 8 applies to replies. Comments that contain prohibited content about any person are hidden, not answered.

## 9. Footage and copyright

The channel uses third-party footage under the criticism, review and reporting exceptions of the Copyright Act 2023, and keeps its use narrow enough that the exception clearly applies.

- **Preferred sources, in order.** Sangsad Television and parliamentary archives; Bangladesh Television and Bangladesh Betar; the speaker's own official page or party channel; press conferences recorded by multiple outlets; private broadcasters last.
- **Short excerpts only.** No single clip exceeds 45 seconds, no piece uses more than 25 percent of its running time as third-party footage, and every clip is followed by original analysis before the next clip.
- **Attribution on screen.** Every clip shows the source outlet or page, the programme or event, and the date, for the full duration of the clip.
- **Private broadcaster footage.** Where a private channel's recording is the only one available, the channel seeks written permission first, and otherwise uses the shortest possible excerpt. A Content ID claim on YouTube is disputed under the criticism exception only after the legal reviewer confirms the piece meets every rule above.
- **No full re-uploads.** The channel never posts a speech, interview or broadcast in full or in substantial part, even with commentary.
- **Own material.** Presenter segments, graphics, document displays and voice-over are original and form the majority of every piece, which also satisfies the platforms' reused-content policies.
- **Music and images.** Only licensed or original music and graphics. No stock images of any real person outside the quoted footage.
- **Platform downloads.** Footage is captured from the original source, not from re-uploads or reaction videos, so the chain of custody for every quote is documented in the verification file.

## 10. Pre-publication checklist

No piece is published until every box is ticked by the named person. The researcher cannot be the checker; the checker cannot be the legal reviewer. For a one-person channel, the legal reviewer is the retained advocate and the checker is a trusted second reader who has signed this constitution.

**Researcher**

- [ ] Verification file complete (Article 5)
- [ ] Every clip under 45 seconds and sourced from the original upload (Article 9)
- [ ] Transcript matches the audio word for word

**Checker**

- [ ] Every factual finding traced to its source document and the page re-read
- [ ] Every sentence tested against the Article 4 table, including title, thumbnail and captions
- [ ] No motive, intent, character or prohibited subject anywhere in the piece (Articles 3, 8)
- [ ] Each fallacy label meets all four conditions of Article 6
- [ ] Speaker's own figure and the document's figure measure the same thing

**Legal reviewer**

- [ ] Piece read in full against the current text of the cyber law and the Penal Code defamation sections
- [ ] Any sentence that could be read as a statement about a person rather than a statement is cut
- [ ] Election-period rules applied if a schedule is in force (Article 7)
- [ ] Sign-off recorded with date in the verification file

**Publisher**

- [ ] Pinned comment posted with the list of sources and a link to the corrections policy
- [ ] Comment filters active for prohibited terms; moderation rota assigned for the first 24 hours
- [ ] Master file and verification file backed up off-platform before posting

A piece that fails any item returns to the researcher. There is no expedited path.

## 11. Corrections and right of reply

The channel's credibility is its only asset, and a visible correction protects it better than a quiet edit. A prompt correction is also the strongest practical answer to a defamation complaint.

- **Right of reply before publication.** Where a piece finds a statement inaccurate, the speaker's office is sent the finding and the sources by email at least 48 hours before publication and invited to respond. Any response is included in full or summarised fairly, and the email is kept in the verification file. No response is recorded as "no response by [date]", never as a refusal.
- **Error reports.** Anyone may report an error through a published address. Every report is logged, acknowledged within 24 hours, and resolved within 72 hours.
- **Corrections are public.** A confirmed error is corrected by a pinned comment and an on-screen card on the original piece, a correction note in the description, and, where the error changes the finding, a new piece stating the correction. The original is not silently edited or deleted; deletion hides the correction and looks like concealment.
- **Takedown on request of the speaker.** If the speaker or their office disputes a finding with a source, publication is paused, the source is checked, and the piece is corrected or restored within 72 hours with the reason stated.
- **Corrections log.** A public page lists every correction with date, piece, what was wrong and what was changed.
- **No defensiveness.** A correction says what was wrong and what the record shows. It never argues with the person who reported it.

## 12. Legal notice, takedown and arrest protocol

The protocol is written down because the first hours after a notice or an arrest are when people make the mistakes that cost the most.

**On receiving a legal notice, police contact or platform government-request notice**

1. Do not reply, post, comment or delete anything. Forward the notice to the retained advocate within one hour.
2. The advocate, not the channel, decides whether to respond, pause the piece or geo-restrict it. Pausing is not an admission and is the default while a notice is reviewed.
3. Preserve the verification file for the piece and send a copy to the advocate and to the off-site backup.
4. Inform the team by the agreed channel. Nobody speaks to press, police or the complainant without the advocate.
5. Log the notice: date received, sender, piece concerned, action taken, date resolved.

**On a platform takedown or government removal order**

- Comply with the removal on the platform concerned while the advocate reviews. Do not re-upload the same piece on another platform or account.
- Appeal through the platform's own process only on the advocate's advice.
- The off-platform master is kept but not republished until cleared.

**On the arrest or detention of a team member**

- The advocate is called first, before family or colleagues are informed publicly.
- The channel posts nothing about the arrest until the advocate approves the wording. A statement, if any, states the facts of the detention and nothing about the complaint, the complainant or the police.
- Pending pieces about the complainant's party are frozen until the matter is resolved, to avoid any appearance of retaliation.
- The bail fund (Article 13) is released to the advocate on request.

**Standing arrangements**

- A retained advocate with cyber-law experience, with a signed engagement letter, a 24-hour number, and a copy of this constitution.
- A second contact at a press-freedom organisation, with the advocate's consent to involve them.
- Every team member carries the advocate's number and knows that the only sentence to say at a police station is that they will answer questions with their lawyer present.

## 13. Records, retention and security

The verification file is the channel's evidence that every claim was checked. It is kept where a seizure of one laptop or one account cannot destroy it.

- **Retention.** Verification files, masters, correspondence and the corrections log are kept for six years from publication, which exceeds the limitation period for the offences in Article 2.
- **Three copies.** Every file exists on the working machine, on an encrypted drive held by a different person, and in cloud storage outside Bangladesh under an account not tied to the channel's public identity.
- **Access.** Two-factor authentication on every platform account and every storage account. No shared passwords; a password manager with named access. Page and channel admin roles held by at least two people so that one arrest does not lock the channel.
- **Devices.** Full-disk encryption on every device. Working footage and drafts are not kept on phones.
- **Communication.** Editorial discussion happens on an end-to-end encrypted service with disappearing messages off for decisions and on for drafts. Sources and tips are never discussed on Facebook Messenger or SMS.
- **Financial record.** A bail and legal fund equal to at least three months of operating cost is kept separately, with the advocate holding signing authority alongside the channel owner. All funding sources are recorded and published annually.
- **Identity.** The decision whether presenters appear under their legal names is made once, before launch, recorded here, and not changed casually. Whatever the decision, the registered owner of the platform accounts is a named person who has read Article 12.

## 14. Governance and amendment

This constitution binds every person and every automated tool that produces content for the channel. It is amended only by the process below, so that no deadline, no viral moment and no single person can loosen it.

- **Owner.** The channel owner is responsible for compliance and signs every amendment.
- **Amendment.** An amendment is proposed in writing, reviewed by the retained advocate, and takes effect seven days after the advocate's written approval. Articles 3, 4, 5 and 8 may be tightened by the owner alone but loosened only with the advocate's approval.
- **Mandatory review triggers.** The constitution is reviewed within 14 days of any of: an amendment to the cyber law; a new Election Commission media code; passage of a National Media Commission law; any legal notice received by the channel; any correction that changed a finding.
- **Annual review.** Once a year the owner, the advocate and one outside reader with no stake in the channel reread the whole text against the year's pieces and corrections.
- **Agents and tools.** Any AI system used for research, drafting, transcription, captioning or moderation is given this constitution as a standing instruction and is configured to refuse output that breaks Articles 3, 4, 6 or 8. Its output is a draft; a person signs every checklist item in Article 10. See AGENTS.md in this repository.
- **Publication.** The current version is public, with its version number and date, so viewers and subjects can hold the channel to it.
